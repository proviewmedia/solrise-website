The Beaufort wind scale: what wind speeds feel like

The Beaufort wind scale describes wind by what it does, on a scale from 0 (Calm) to 12 (Hurricane force). For example, 19 to 24 mph is a Fresh breeze, when small trees in leaf begin to sway, and 39 to 46 mph is a Gale, when walking is difficult.

What the Beaufort scale is

Wind is how fast air is moving near the surface, and which direction it is coming from. A number like "19 mph" is precise but hard to picture. The Beaufort scale fixes that by pairing each range of speeds with what you can actually see happening around you, from smoke rising straight up to trees being uprooted.

Why wind changes

Wind picks up when there is a bigger difference in pressure between two nearby areas: the atmosphere is just trying to even that out. That is why a strong storm system, with very low pressure near its center, brings strong wind with it.

Where you stand matters too. Airport wind is measured over open ground, so wooded trails and city streets usually feel calmer, while ridges and open beaches feel stronger. Coastal wind can also differ a lot from inland forecasts because of the water.

What wind means for you

Everyday

Above about 25 mph, umbrellas turn inside out and loose patio furniture becomes a problem. If you are heading out for a run or a walk, a headwind on the way out is a tailwind on the way back, so expect your easy effort to feel slower into the wind.

Outdoors

Start runs and rides into the wind so the tailwind helps on the tired half home. Above about 20 mph, ridgelines and exposed summits get much gustier than the trailhead, and crosswinds can push a bike sideways.

Flying

Check the gust spread, not just the average: a big gap between steady wind and gusts is what upsets small aircraft on landing and pushes drones off station. Compare the crosswind component with your aircraft's demonstrated limit, and remember wind is usually stronger at 400 ft than on the ground.

Did you know?

The Beaufort wind scale, still used on marine forecasts today, was devised in 1805 by Royal Navy officer Francis Beaufort to describe wind by its effect on the sea.

Common questions

How strong is a 20 mph wind?

20 mph is a Fresh breeze, Beaufort force 5 (19 to 24 mph): small trees in leaf begin to sway.

What wind speed is a gale?

On the Beaufort scale, a Gale is force 8, 39 to 46 mph (63 to 75 km/h). Twigs break off trees and walking is difficult.

What wind speed is too strong for an umbrella?

Above about 25 mph, a Strong breeze on the Beaufort scale, umbrellas are hard to use and often turn inside out.
